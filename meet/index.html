Plain answers

Who pays for this site?

Nobody pays to appear on it. The site runs no advertising of its own and has no donors. It is published on GitHub's free public hosting.

Does this site track me?

This site counts nothing about its readers: no page sends a visit, a click or a place anywhere, except through the two choices below. There is no account and no sign-in. Your reading settings and your companion are kept in this browser, on this device, and are never sent anywhere.

Where a page offers "Use my location", your place is worked out on your device and is not sent anywhere. Every page takes its type from this site. Three things come from other servers. The opening picture of Plain Congress shows the weather at the Capitol in Washington, from Open-Meteo; that request carries the Capitol's place, never yours. If you switch on street maps on a ballot page, OpenStreetMap's servers see which map squares are asked for, and the page says so. And if you type your address on a ballot page and press Look up, your browser sends it straight to the U.S. Census Bureau's address finder, which sends back where it is; this site never sees the address or keeps it.

What happens when I share a page?

A Share button sends a link. On a phone it opens your phone’s own share sheet; on a computer, a short list of places, such as e-mail, Facebook or LinkedIn, or Copy link. Nothing is sent until you pick a place. The place you pick then sees the link and the short message with it. This site loads nothing from those services and never learns whom you share with. If a link would show roughly where you vote, the page says so first. The Access page says more.

Can I get it as an app?

Yes. You can keep this site on your phone, tablet or computer, installed from the site itself: free, with no app store and no account, and it updates itself. Pages you have opened, and a ballot you kept, still work with no connection. Installing sends nothing to this site; on Android, your browser’s maker (Google, for Chrome) builds the app from the site’s public description of itself. Get the app shows how.

Why should I trust the ratings?

You do not have to take them on trust. Facts come from official records and link back to them. Ratings are judgments, kept apart and labelled. Each one shows its evidence grade, its reasons, its sources and the version of the rubric it was made under.

So far ten bills carry a full set of ratings. They were applied by hand, by Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, as a preview, and each says so. Who backed a bill is worked out from the recorded votes by a published formula. The Method page has the whole rubric.

Reading & access

Kept on this device only. Nothing is sent anywhere. Turn on as many as help: they combine. Where two differ, the larger text, the more space, the less motion and the quieter companion win. Otherwise the one you chose last wins.
