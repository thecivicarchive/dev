Sandbox example

Into the notebook

The short scene that plays the first time in a visit that you follow a notebook link from one of the layout pages: in the warm light of a reading room a leather notebook lies on the desk; your eyes settle over it, the cover opens, a few leaves turn, and the page you asked for lies flat, filling your view. About three and a half seconds; then the notebook page itself. Later in the same visit a notebook link is a quick fade.

Try it

Or follow a real notebook link, the way the layout pages carry them: The count: North Carolina’s U.S. Senate race. The first time this visit it plays the scene; after that it fades. A click, a tap or any key skips it. “Play it” plays the scene over this page and comes back to it.

Key frames

How it behaves

  • The first notebook link of a visitThe full scene, about 3.6 seconds, then the page. Any click, tap or key skips it at once; a Skip button can be reached by keyboard. The browser’s own keys (Ctrl, Alt or Cmd chords, the function keys) pass through.
  • Later links in the same visitA quick fade into the page, nothing to skip.
  • Motion not on full, Calm on, or your device’s own reduced motionOne still picture of the open page, then a plain fade: nothing moves.
  • Save-Data on, no WebGL2, or files slower than 2.5 secondsA plain fade. A slow load leaves the scene for the next way in to try again.
  • Back, then ForwardNever replays: Back while it loads or plays stops it, and Forward brings the page back as it was.
  • Ctrl, Cmd, Shift or middle clicks; links to a new tabLeft alone: the browser does what it always does.
  • While it playsThe scene covers the page in its own layer with a name for assistive technology (“On the way to the notebook”); the page behind is marked inert until it ends.
  • What leaves your deviceNothing. Every file comes with the page; nothing loads from another server, and nothing is sent.

How it is made

The notebook is built in its real size (148 by 210 millimetres, 18 millimetres of pages) from physically based materials with photographed colour, surface and roughness maps: the leather tinted and tiled at a binding’s finer grain, the title pressed into it in brass foil, the varnished desk under a thin clear coat, the paper with its fibre and tooth. The light of a photographed room is prefiltered on your device for reflections and fill, and the room shows blurred behind the desk; a warm desk lamp and a cool window are the two lights, with soft shadows. The cover and every leaf are real geometry: each leaf is a bent sheet whose shape is worked out every frame (the fold leads, the free edge trails, the corners curl a little) and which comes to rest in the gutter’s valley on either side. The picture reaches the screen through a lens: a high-range picture with depth, a soft bloom, depth of field with the focus racking from the cover to the page, a filmic curve, a quiet warm grade, the lens’s fall-off at the edges, a very fine grain, and the veil it fades in from and out to.

The scene’s own files beside the page: about 3.1 MB on a computer and 1.4 MB on a phone (textures at 512 pixels, fewer segments, no depth of field), fetched on the first hover, focus or tap of a notebook link.

What still falls short of a photograph: the lamp’s light does not bounce (the underside of a turning leaf is lit by the room’s light alone), the paper is opaque but for a ghost of the next page drawn into it, the pen and the lamps are plain shapes, and the room behind is a photograph of another room, seen out of focus.

Plain answers

Who pays for this site?

Nobody pays to appear on it. The site runs no advertising of its own and has no donors. It is published on GitHub's free public hosting.

Does this site track me?

This site counts nothing about its readers: no page sends a visit, a click or a place anywhere, except as described below. There is no account and no sign-in. Your reading settings and your companion are kept in this browser, on this device, and are never sent anywhere.

Where a page offers "Use my location", your place is worked out on your device and is not sent anywhere. Every page takes its type from this site. Three things come from other servers. The opening picture of Plain Congress shows the weather at the Capitol in Washington, from Open-Meteo; that request carries the Capitol's place, never yours. If you switch on street maps on a ballot or Forecasts page, OpenStreetMap's servers see which map squares are asked for, and the page says so. And if you type your address on a ballot or Forecasts page and press Look up, your browser sends it straight to the U.S. Census Bureau's address finder, which sends back where it is; this site never sees the address or keeps it.

What happens when I share a page?

A Share button sends a link. On a phone it opens your phone’s own share sheet; on a computer, a short list of places, such as e-mail, Facebook or LinkedIn, or Copy link. Nothing is sent until you pick a place. The place you pick then sees the link and the short message with it. This site loads nothing from those services and never learns whom you share with. If a link would show roughly where you vote, the page says so first. The Access page says more.

Can I get it as an app?

Yes. You can keep this site on your phone, tablet or computer, installed from the site itself: free, with no app store and no account, and it updates itself. Pages you have opened, and a ballot you kept, still work with no connection. Installing sends nothing to this site; on Android, your browser’s maker (Google, for Chrome) builds the app from the site’s public description of itself. Get the app shows how.

Why should I trust the ratings?

You do not have to take them on trust. Facts come from official records and link back to them. Ratings are judgments, kept apart and labelled. Each one shows its evidence grade, its reasons, its sources and the version of the rubric it was made under.

So far ten bills carry a full set of ratings. They were applied by hand, by Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, as a preview, and each says so. Who backed a bill is worked out from the recorded votes by a published formula. The Method page has the whole rubric.

Reading & access

Kept on this device only. Nothing is sent anywhere. Turn on as many as help: they combine. Where two differ, the larger text, the more space, the less motion and the quieter companion win. Otherwise the one you chose last wins.
